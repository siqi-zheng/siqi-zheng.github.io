Summer teaching always has its own rhythm. Attendance can be uneven, the pace can feel compressed, and students often engage with the course in very different ways from a regular term. That context matters for interpreting this term's course evaluations.

This time, 8 of 52 students submitted evaluations, for a 15% response rate. That is a small sample, so these comments and ratings are best understood as a useful window into how a subset of students experienced the course, not as a complete picture of the whole class.

Even with that limitation, the feedback was strikingly consistent, and the comparative numbers are the real headline this term: on almost every item, this course outperformed both the department and the division, sometimes by a wide margin.

# TL;DR The course consistently outperformed departmental and divisional benchmarks

Across all nine rating-scale items with comparative data, __this course beat the department average every single time and beat the division average in eight of nine__. Here's the full picture:

- Intellectually stimulating: 4.5 vs. 4.2 (dept), 4.2 (div)
- Deeper understanding of subject matter: 4.4 vs. 4.2 (dept), 4.3 (div)
- Atmosphere conducive to learning: 4.6 vs. 4.1 (dept), 4.2 (div)
- Assignments/tests improved understanding: 4.3 vs. 4.0 (dept), 4.1 (div)
- Assignments/tests demonstrated understanding: 4.5 vs. 4.0 (dept), 4.1 (div)
- Overall learning experience: 4.4 vs. 3.8 (dept), 4.0 (div)
- Enthusiasm for learning: 4.6 vs. 4.1 (dept), 4.3 (div)
- Recommend to other students: 4.1 vs. 3.7 (dept), 3.9 (div)
- Overall quality of instruction: 4.6 vs. 4.0 (dept)

The gap on "overall learning experience" (4.4 vs. 3.8 departmentally) is a 0.6-point spread, which is large on a 5-point scale. The gap on "overall quality of instruction" (4.6 vs. 4.0) tells a similar story. These are not marginal differences; they suggest the course was operating at a meaningfully different level than the typical departmental offering this term.

The Institutional Composite Mean (the average of the five core institutional items) came out to 4.5 for this course, compared with 4.1 for the department and 4.2 for the division. That composite is designed to smooth out noise across individual items, so the fact that it still shows a clear gap is a good sign that this wasn't a fluke driven by one aspect of the course.

## What seemed to drive the gap

The two largest gaps were both about the learning environment I created rather than about course content per se: atmosphere conducive to learning (4.6 vs. 4.1 dept) and enthusiasm for learning (4.6 vs. 4.1 dept). These are the items most directly under an instructor's control, independent of course design inherited from previous terms, so it's meaningful that they showed the strongest performance relative to peers.

The written comments reinforced this. One student noted that proofs and computations were made 

> comprehensive and teachable while still delivering the core of the subject 

which captures exactly the balance I aim for: rigor without unnecessary intimidation.

## Where the margin was smaller

The one item where the course-vs-division gap nearly disappeared was Ins02 (deeper understanding of subject matter): 4.4 course vs. 4.3 division, though still ahead of the department's 4.2. This is also the item most tied to course content and structure rather than classroom delivery, and it lines up with the qualitative comment about mismatches between old STA255 material and the current version of the course in lectures and quizzes.

The recommendation item (FAS003) also showed a smaller edge relative to division (4.1 vs. 3.9) than most other items, though still a healthy margin over the department (3.7). Students rated the teaching itself higher than they rated the overall "package" of the course experience, which is consistent with the idea that content alignment, not instruction quality, is the main thing holding back an even stronger set of numbers.

## Workload: heavy, but apparently earned

Workload came in above both benchmarks (3.8 vs. 3.5 dept, 3.3 div), meaning students found this course more demanding than average. Combined with the strong ratings elsewhere, this doesn't read as "too hard", it reads as "rigorous and worth it." Rigor is easier for students to embrace when it's paired with the kind of instructional quality this term's scores reflect.

# Key Takeaways

## What I am taking from this term

The clearest message from the data is that the instructional core of this course, the teaching itself, is performing well above departmental and divisional norms. That's the throughline across atmosphere, enthusiasm, and overall instructional quality, and it shows up consistently regardless of which comparison group I use.

The place to focus next is tightening the smaller number of areas where the course still lands closer to (rather than clearly above) its benchmarks: content alignment between lecture materials and assessments, and making sure the "STA255 legacy content" issue mentioned in the comments gets fully resolved before the next offering.

## What I want to improve next time

Going forward, I want to audit the full path students move through: lecture content, quizzes, assignments, and any leftover materials from earlier versions of the course, to eliminate the disjuncts the comment pointed to. I also want to gather feedback earlier in the term, since a mid-course check-in would let me catch alignment issues while there's still time to fix them for the students in the room.

Given how strong the comparative numbers already are on the instructional side, the marginal returns now are highest on course design coherence rather than on teaching delivery itself.

# Closing reflection

This term's evaluations, small sample notwithstanding, tell a consistent story: the course outperformed both departmental and divisional benchmarks on effectively every measured dimension, with the largest gaps precisely on the items most tied to instructional quality. The next step is making the course structure catch up to that strength, so the experience feels as coherent as the teaching within it.
